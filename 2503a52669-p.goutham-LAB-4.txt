Q1:
Customer Email Classification
A company receives a large number of customer emails every day and wants to automatically classify them into the following categories:
•	Billing
•	Technical Support
•	Feedback
•	Others
Instead of training a new machine learning model, the company decides to use prompt engineering techniques with an existing large language model.
Tasks
1.	Prepare five short sample emails, each belonging to one of the above categories.
2.	Write a zero-shot prompt to classify a given email into one of the categories without providing any examples.
3.	Write a one-shot prompt by including one labeled email example and ask the model to classify a new email.
4.	Write a few-shot prompt by including two or three labeled email examples and ask the model to classify a new email.
5.	Compare the outputs obtained using zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot prompting techniques and briefly comment on their effectiveness
PROMPT:
Complete this Customer Email Classification assignment.
Categories: Billing, Technical Support, Feedback, Others
1.	Create 5 short sample emails and label each category.
2.	Write a zero-shot prompt and show its output for a new email.
3.	Write a one-shot prompt with 1 labeled example and show its output for a new email.
4.	Write a few-shot prompt with 3 labeled examples and show its output for a new email.
5.	Give a short comparison of zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot based on accuracy, advantages, and limitations.
Keep everything simple, clear, and college-assignment ready.
OUTPUT:
\# Customer Email Classification

## Categories

- Billing
- Technical Support
- Feedback
- Others

## 1. Sample Emails

| No. | Email | Category |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why was I charged twice for my subscription? | Billing |
| 2 | The application crashes whenever I try to log in. | Technical Support |
| 3 | Your new dashboard is easy to use and looks great. | Feedback |
| 4 | What are your office working hours? | Others |
| 5 | Please send me an invoice for last month. | Billing |

## 2. Zero-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**

```text
Classify the following customer email into exactly one category: Billing, Technical Support, Feedback, or Others.

Email: "I cannot reset my password because the reset link does not work."
Return only the category.
```

**Output:** `Technical Support`

## 3. One-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**

```text
Classify customer emails into exactly one category: Billing, Technical Support, Feedback, or Others.

Example:
Email: "The monthly fee on my account is incorrect."
Category: Billing

Now classify this email:
Email: "The website is very slow when I open my account."
Return only the category.
```

**Output:** `Technical Support`

## 4. Few-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**

```text
Classify customer emails into exactly one category: Billing, Technical Support, Feedback, or Others.

Example 1:
Email: "I need a refund for a duplicate payment."
Category: Billing

Example 2:
Email: "The mobile app closes when I upload a document."
Category: Technical Support

Example 3:
Email: "The service is helpful, but the search feature could be improved."
Category: Feedback

Now classify this email:
Email: "Can I change the email address on my account?"
Return only the category.
```

**Output:** `Others`

## 5. Comparison

| Method | Accuracy | Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot | Usually moderate | Fast and needs no examples | May misunderstand category boundaries |
| One-shot | Better than zero-shot | Shows the expected format and one classification pattern | One example may not represent every type of email |
| Few-shot | Usually the highest | Multiple examples improve consistency and understanding | Takes more prompt space and requires good examples |

**Conclusion:** Few-shot prompting is generally the most reliable for this task, while zero-shot prompting is the quickest and simplest.

# Student Feedback Analysis

## 1. Zero-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**
```text
Classify the following student feedback as Positive, Negative, or Neutral.
Feedback: "The instructor explained the topics clearly, and the classes were very helpful."
Reply with only the classification.
```

**Output:** `Positive`

## 2. One-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**
```text
Classify student feedback as Positive, Negative, or Neutral.

Example:
Feedback: "The course materials were organized and useful."
```


## 4. How Examples Improve Accuracy

Examples show the model what each label means and how to apply the labels to real feedback. They clarify the difference between positive, negative, and neutral statements. Multiple examples also help the model classify mixed or less obvious opinions more consistently.

Social Media Post Moderation
A social media platform wants to classify posts into Acceptable, Offensive, or Spam.
Questions:
a) Write a Zero-shot prompt for post moderation.
b) Convert it into a One-shot prompt.
c) Design a Few-shot prompt using multiple examples.
d) Explain the challenges of Zero-shot prompting in content moderation.
PROMPT:
Solve this Social Media Post Moderation assignment.
Classify posts as Acceptable, Offensive, or Spam.
Provide:
1.	A Zero-shot prompt with its output.
2.	A One-shot prompt with 1 labeled example and its output.
3.	A Few-shot prompt with 3 labeled examples and its output.
4.	A short explanation of the challenges of Zero-shot prompting in content moderation.
Keep it simple, clear, and college-assignment ready.
OUTPUT:
 # Social Media Post Moderation

## Task

Classify the following post as **Acceptable**, **Offensive**, or **Spam**.

Post: “I really enjoyed today’s workshop. Thank you to everyone who organized it!”

## 1. Zero-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**

> Classify this social media post as **Acceptable**, **Offensive**, or **Spam**. Give a brief reason.
>
> Post: “I really enjoyed today’s workshop. Thank you to everyone who organized it!”

**Output:**

> **Acceptable** — The post is polite, relevant, and does not contain harmful or promotional content.

## 2. One-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**

> Classify each social media post as **Acceptable**, **Offensive**, or **Spam**. Give a brief reason.
>
> Example: “Congratulations on your new job!” → **Acceptable** — It is a positive and harmless message.
>
> Post: “I really enjoyed today’s workshop. Thank you to everyone who organized it!”

**Output:**

> **Acceptable** — The post is positive, respectful, and relevant.

## 3. Few-Shot Prompt

**Prompt:**

> Classify each social media post as **Acceptable**, **Offensive**, or **Spam**. Use the examples as a guide and give a brief reason.
>
> Example 1: “The sunset looks beautiful today.” → **Acceptable** — It is harmless and appropriate.
>
> Example 2: “You are stupid and nobody wants you here.” → **Offensive** — It insults and targets another person.
>
> Example 3: “Click this link now to win a free phone! Share with 20 friends!” → **Spam** — It is unsolicited promotional content with suspicious engagement requests.
>
> Post: “I really enjoyed today’s workshop. Thank you to everyone who organized it!”

**Output:**

> **Acceptable** — The post is respectful, relevant, and not abusive or promotional.

## 4. Challenges of Zero-Shot Prompting in Content Moderation

Zero-shot prompting can be difficult because the model receives no examples to clarify the category boundaries. Words may have different meanings depending on context, culture, or slang. Sarcasm, jokes, coded insults, and indirect harassment can also be misunderstood. In addition, it may be unclear whether advertising is spam or a legitimate recommendation. These issues can lead to inconsistent classifications, false positives, or harmful posts being missed.
